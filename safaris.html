Our Safari Experiences

Birding Safaris

Uganda is home to over 1,000 bird species. Our birding safaris take you through wetlands, forests, and savannas to spot the elusive Shoebill, Great Blue Turaco, African Green Broadbill, and hundreds more.

Ultimate Wildlife Safaris

Encounter the Big Five and beyond across Uganda's premier national parks. From tree-climbing lions in Ishasha to herds of elephants in Queen Elizabeth NP, experience East Africa's most iconic wildlife.

Primate Safaris

Track mountain gorillas in Bwindi Impenetrable Forest and chimpanzees in Kibale. Uganda is one of the best places on Earth for primate encounters, home to 13 primate species.

Sport Fishing Safaris

Cast your line into the legendary waters of the Nile and Lake Victoria. Pursue the mighty Nile Perch and Tilapia while surrounded by stunning landscapes and abundant birdlife.

Cultural Safaris

Immerse yourself in Uganda's rich cultural tapestry. Visit the Batwa pygmies, the Karamojong warriors, and royal kingdoms. Experience traditional dance, crafts, and ceremonies that have endured for centuries.

Destinations Across East Africa

Uganda

The Pearl of africa

Uganda, the Pearl of Africa as defined by Sir Winston Churchill in the 1930s (locally translated as "Uganda Enkkula"), is an emerald endowed with beautiful landscapes, culture, wildlife, and the most welcoming people. Bordered by Rwanda, D.R. Congo, Tanzania, Kenya, and South Sudan, it is a cradle of primates—home to Mountain Gorillas, Golden Monkeys, and Chimpanzees. In the Kazinga Channel, Uganda boasts the highest concentration of Hippos in Africa. Protected areas include 10 national parks and 6 wildlife reserves. Water bodies cover nearly one-third of the country, including Lake Victoria and the source of the River Nile in Jinja. Highlights include whitewater rafting, hiking Mt. Rwenzori, and scaling the Virunga volcanoes (Gahinga, Sabinyo, and Mufumbira).

Rwanda

The Land of a Thousand Hills

Rwanda, also known as the Land of a Thousand Hills, is decorated by stunning rolling hills and a border with Uganda and Congo formed by a chain of volcanoes forming scenic splendor like no other. Today, Rwanda is one of the premier primate tracking destinations and offers exceptional luxury eco-experiences. Volcanoes National Park offers gorilla tracking, golden monkey tracking, and volcano hiking. Nyungwe National Park is home to 13 primate species including chimpanzees, black-and-white colobus, and Sykes monkeys. Akagera National Park features savannah species including elephants, zebras, giraffes, buffalo, lions, leopards, and rhinos, making it an African Big Five habitat.

Kenya

The Pride of Africa

Kenya is the classic and most celebrated safari destination. On a contemporary safari, you can lose yourself for days, untroubled by crowds, communing with the bush and its inhabitants in an almost spiritual experience. The best time to visit Kenya is during the dry season from late June to October. The wildebeest migration usually reaches Masai Mara by August and remains until October before moving toward Serengeti in Tanzania. Wildlife viewing remains rewarding throughout the year across Kenya's diverse ecosystems.

Tanzania

The Land of Kilimanjaro

The sheer scale of Tanzania makes a single visit unforgettable. The annual wildebeest and zebra migration in the Serengeti National Park easily fills a week of your holiday. Climbing Kilimanjaro—Africa's highest free-standing mountain—is an iconic expedition. Other remote and fascinating parks include Tarangire, Katavi, Ruaha, and Selous. The Ngorongoro Crater is a must-see for its dramatic geology and dense wildlife, while the chimpanzee sanctuaries of Mahale in the west and the spice island of Zanzibar offer serene respite.

Essential Safari Knowledge

Gorilla & Chimpanzee Tracking FAQ

Is Gorilla Trekking better in Uganda or Rwanda?

This is a debatable question. The answer is probably that they are as good as each other. Whilst there are no guarantees in either Rwanda or Uganda that gorillas will be sighted, it is extremely rare for clients to be disappointed.

As mountain gorillas forage for food in the forest over fair distances in both countries, the trek can be either easy or strenuous at both locations. If an easier trek is desired, the best chance is during the green seasons (April–May and November), though no guarantees exist in wild nature. Accommodation in Uganda offers a diverse range of premier eco-lodges, while Rwanda also features world-class high-end retreats.

What are the chances of finding the Gorillas?

Whilst you have an exceptionally high chance of finding the gorillas, we cannot guarantee 100% success in the wild. National Park regulations stipulate that if the gorillas have not been found by 1:00 PM, trekkers must turn back to avoid tracking in the dark back to camp.

Refunds are only considered if a client is sick prior to the trek, at the discretion of the Senior Warden at Park Headquarters. No refunds are entertained if clients decide not to trek because they are tired or turn around due to physical fatigue.

Age Requirement: To minimize the risk of transmitting childhood diseases to the gorillas, the lower age limit for tracking is strictly 15 years. This minimum age limit is enforced, and all passport details are verified at Park Headquarters.

What does a typical day on a gorilla trek look like?

After breakfast, we take a short drive to Park Headquarters and meet with local guides and trackers who give an essential briefing on the etiquette of gorilla trekking. We then hike into the forest to where the gorillas were seen the evening before and track from that point.

The trek can take from 1 to 6 hours and reach altitudes up to 7,500 feet. The terrain is rough and at times muddy. Although the hike is physically demanding, all fatigue is forgotten once the gorillas are located—an encounter widely described as one of the most profound natural history experiences in the world.

Packed picnic lunches and plenty of drinking water are provided. Rain can fall at a few minutes' notice, so waterproof gear and zip-lock bags for cameras are essential.

What clothing should I wear and what should I pack?

Footwear: Strong hiking boots with good tread and ankle support are essential. Gardening gloves and gaiters are useful optional extras against nettles and brush.

Clothing: Wear a long-sleeve top to protect your arms against thorns and sturdy long pants (tuck the ends into your socks to prevent safari ants). Dress in layers so you can peel off outer layers as you warm up. Pack a fleece/sweater and a spare top in your daypack.

Weather Gear & Photography: Waterproof clothing in muted natural colors is essential. Bring zip-lock bags for cameras and electronics. Note: Flash photography is strictly prohibited.

Porters: Porters are available for hire at park headquarters and are highly recommended. Hiring a porter provides vital income for local families while giving you valuable physical assistance on steep muddy sections.

Safety on the trek – are the guides armed?

All trekking groups are accompanied by Uganda Wildlife Authority park guides, expert trackers, porters, and armed security personnel.

This is standard precautionary protocol across East African national parks, ensuring guest safety in forests inhabited by wild forest elephants and buffalo. Guides carry hand-held radios and remain in constant communication with park headquarters and other trekking parties.

What is Chimpanzee tracking like in Uganda?

Chimpanzee tracking is an exhilarating, fast-paced primate encounter. Uganda features four official tracking destinations: Budongo in Murchison Falls NP, Kyambura Gorge in Queen Elizabeth NP, Kalinzu Forest, and the world-famous Kibale National Park.

Kibale National Park boasts the largest primate population in Africa with 13 resident primate species. Chimpanzees are highly vocal and active; during your trek, you will witness grooming, foraging, energetic displays, and vocal calls ringing through the canopy. Communities can range from small troops to over a hundred individuals.

Why are gorilla trekking permits strictly regulated?

Only about 880 mountain gorillas survive on our fragile planet, with approximately 320 in Uganda's Bwindi and 380 across the Virunga Massif. They face pressures from habitat loss, human disease transmission, and poaching.

Strict visitor limits (maximum 8 visitors per gorilla family per day for exactly one hour) minimize stress and disease exposure. The permit revenue directly funds 24/7 ranger anti-poaching patrols, veterinary care, and community revenue-sharing projects around the park boundaries.
