Frequently Asked Questions

Everything you need to know about planning an authentic East African journey—from gorilla permit costs and seasonal timing to health, safety, and packing essentials.

1. Gorilla & Chimpanzee Trekking

Permit fees, Bwindi vs Volcanoes comparison, trekking expectations, and habituation experiences.

How much does a gorilla trekking permit cost in Uganda for US and international travelers?

For foreign non-residents (including US, UK, Canadian, and EU travelers), a standard Uganda gorilla trekking permit costs $800 USD per person.

During Uganda Wildlife Authority (UWA) promotional low-season months (April, May, and November), permits are discounted to $600 USD per person. Your permit guarantees one full hour with a habituated mountain gorilla family in Bwindi Impenetrable or Mgahinga Gorilla National Park, including park entry fees and armed ranger guides for the trek.

Is Uganda or Rwanda better for gorilla trekking?

Uganda offers significantly better value and biodiversity for most travelers. A Uganda gorilla permit is $800 USD compared to $1,500 USD in Rwanda—a $700 per-person savings that allows you to upgrade your lodges or extend your safari.

While Rwanda’s Volcanoes National Park offers shorter transfers from Kigali (2.5 hours), Uganda’s Bwindi Impenetrable Forest protects nearly half of the world’s remaining mountain gorillas, offers the exclusive 4-hour Gorilla Habituation Experience, and pairs seamlessly with classic Big Five savannah safaris.

What is it actually like to trek mountain gorillas in Bwindi Impenetrable Forest?

Your day begins with a 7:30 AM briefing at the park headquarters before setting off into Bwindi’s mist-covered rainforest with an armed UWA ranger and expert trackers. Treks last anywhere from 2 to 6 hours across steep, muddy, and untamed mountain slopes at elevations between 1,160m and 2,607m.

Once your trackers locate the gorilla family, you spend a magical, uninterrupted 60 minutes observing silverbacks, mothers, and juveniles just meters away. Hiring a local porter ($15–$20 USD) is strongly recommended to carry your daypack and assist on steep terrain.

What is the Chimpanzee Habituation Experience, and how is it different from standard chimp trekking?

Standard chimpanzee trekking in Kibale Forest National Park gives you 1 hour with an already-habituated troop. The Chimpanzee Habituation Experience (CHEX) is a full-day immersive expedition where you accompany researchers and rangers from dawn until dusk—watching chimps wake up, de-nest, forage, patrol territory, and build new nests for the night.

CHEX permits cost $300 USD per person and are limited to very small groups, making it ideal for photographers and primate enthusiasts.

3. Itineraries, Parks & Timing

Multi-country routes, hidden wilderness gems, optimal seasons, and booking lead times.

What is the best multi-country safari itinerary combining Uganda, Rwanda, and Kenya?

A 10 to 14-day East Africa circuit is the gold standard for combining primate trekking with the Great Migration.

A seamless route begins in Nairobi, Kenya (3–4 nights in the Maasai Mara for classic Big Five game drives), followed by a regional flight to Entebbe, Uganda (2–3 nights in Kibale Forest for chimpanzees and Queen Elizabeth National Park for tree-climbing lions), driving south into Bwindi Impenetrable Forest for mountain gorilla trekking, and concluding with a scenic cross-border transfer to Kigali, Rwanda (with an optional second trek in Volcanoes National Park) for your international flight home.

Is Kidepo Valley National Park worth visiting for a first-time traveler?

Yes—if you want raw, crowd-free African wilderness. Located in Uganda’s remote northeastern corner bordering Kenya and South Sudan, Kidepo Valley is widely regarded as East Africa’s finest off-the-beaten-path savannah park.

You will encounter massive buffalo herds, lions resting on granite kopjes, cheetahs, ostriches, and giraffes without another safari vehicle in sight. To avoid long 10-hour drives, Imoratok Safaris arranges short 2-hour scheduled bush flights from Entebbe straight into Kidepo.

When is the best time of year to visit Uganda for a safari and gorilla trekking?

Uganda is an exceptional year-round destination straddling the equator:

  • Peak Dry Seasons (June–August & December–February): Best for clear savannah game viewing, easier hiking conditions in Bwindi, and minimal rainfall.
  • Green & Shoulder Seasons (March–May & September–November): Lush emerald landscapes ideal for photography, newborn wildlife, vibrant birdwatching, fewer crowds, and discounted $600 gorilla permits in April, May, and November.

How far in advance should I book my Uganda safari?

To secure prime lodge availability and your preferred gorilla trekking sector, we recommend booking 6 to 12 months in advance for peak travel months (June–September and December–February).

Because UWA requires full upfront payment to lock in gorilla permits, early booking prevents last-minute accommodation compromises.

4. Pricing, Vehicles & Logistics

Quote inclusions, road conditions, fleet specifications, and on-safari internet connectivity.

What is included in an Imoratok Safaris package quote—and are there hidden fees?

Unlike budget vehicle-only rentals that leave you paying surprise gate fees and fuel surcharges on the ground, our quotes are transparent and all-inclusive.

Your package covers a private custom 4x4 Land Cruiser, a dedicated professional Ugandan driver-guide, all fuel, park entrance fees, scheduled boat cruises and game drives, full-board lodge accommodations, bottled drinking water in the vehicle, and pre-purchased gorilla and chimpanzee permits. International flights, visas, travel insurance, and personal tips are excluded.

What are the road conditions like in Uganda, and should I self-drive?

While main highways connecting major cities are paved and well-maintained, park access roads—particularly the mountainous tracks leading into Bwindi Impenetrable Forest—are unpaved, steep, and can become slick during rain showers. We strongly advise against self-driving.

You will travel in a custom-built, pop-up roof 4x4 Toyota Land Cruiser driven by an experienced local guide who navigates rough terrain effortlessly while spotting wildlife you would otherwise miss.

Will I have reliable Wi-Fi and internet connectivity while on safari?

Most mid-range and luxury safari lodges provide complimentary Wi-Fi in main lounge and dining areas, though bandwidth can be limited in deep forest or remote savannah locations.

If you need consistent connectivity on the road, your guide will assist you in purchasing and registering a local MTN or Airtel 4G/5G SIM card or eSIM upon arrival at Entebbe Airport.

5. Health, Safety, Packing & Etiquette

Tourist security, 2026 entry rules, tipping guidelines, essential gear, and family travel.

Is Uganda safe for American and international tourists?

Yes. Uganda’s national parks, wildlife reserves, and tourism corridors are remarkably safe, stable, and welcoming to international visitors.

All park activities are escorted by trained, armed Uganda Wildlife Authority rangers, and safari lodges maintain 24/7 on-site security. Your private Imoratok Safaris guide remains with you from airport pickup to departure, ensuring smooth, stress-free logistics throughout your journey.

What vaccinations, visas, and health requirements are needed to enter Uganda?

  • Visa: Most travelers (including US, UK, Canadian, and EU citizens) must apply online for a Uganda eVisa ($50 USD) or the East Africa Tourist Visa ($100 USD covering Uganda, Kenya, and Rwanda) prior to departure.
  • Yellow Fever (2026 Update): As of October 2026, Uganda no longer requires proof of Yellow Fever vaccination as a mandatory entry document. However, vaccination is still strongly recommended by the CDC and WHO, and carrying your physical Yellow Card remains essential if combining your trip with Kenya, Tanzania, or Rwanda.
  • Malaria & Health: Consult your travel clinic regarding malaria prophylaxis, and ensure you complete Uganda’s online traveler health declaration form before arrival.

How much should I budget for tipping my safari guide, trackers, and lodge staff?

Tipping is customary in Uganda and deeply appreciated as a direct reward for great service:

  • Private Driver-Guide: $15 to $25 USD per day (total from your travel party, not per person).
  • Gorilla/Chimp Rangers & Trackers: $10 to $20 USD per trek group, split among the ranger team.
  • Gorilla Trekking Porter: $15 to $20 USD per trek.
  • Lodge Staff: $5 to $10 USD per day placed in the lodge’s communal staff tip box upon checkout.

Tip: Bring crisp USD bills printed in 2013 or later, as older or torn bills are rejected by local banks.

What should I pack for a Uganda safari and gorilla trek?

Pack in a soft-sided duffel bag (under 15 kg / 33 lbs if taking domestic bush flights) and stick to neutral earth tones (khaki, olive, brown)—never pack camouflage clothing, as military prints are illegal for civilians in Uganda.

Essentials include broken-in waterproof hiking boots, knee-high gaiters (to keep out mud and safari ants), lightweight long-sleeved shirts and trousers, gardening gloves (for gripping forest vines), a breathable rain jacket, surgical/KN95 face masks (mandatory when viewing primates), and a 200–400mm zoom camera lens without flash.

Are Uganda safaris suitable for families with children or older travelers?

Absolutely. Uganda is wonderful for multi-generational travel, with private vehicles allowing you to set a comfortable pace.

Please note that Uganda Wildlife Authority enforces a strict minimum age of 15 years for gorilla trekking and 12 years for chimpanzee tracking. For families with younger children, we curate custom wildlife itineraries featuring boat cruises on the Kazinga Channel, rhino tracking on foot at Ziwa Rhino Sanctuary, and guided nature walks. For older travelers or those with mobility considerations, Bwindi offers sedan chair ("African helicopter") porter teams who safely carry guests through the forest to see the gorillas.
